PATCH 1 — CURRENT VERIFIED CANADA SNAPSHOT
Reference Date: August 2026 Status: CURRENT REFERENCE ONLY — NOT A 2028 GUARANTEE
This snapshot exists only to answer: What important Canada/Education/Immigration facts are currently true and useful for planning today? It must remain compact and must never replace decision-time revalidation.
1. Public Master’s / Doctoral PAL/TAL exemption
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED Since January 1, 2026, applicants to degree-granting master’s or doctoral programs at public institutions do not need a provincial or territorial attestation letter (PAL/TAL) for the study-permit application. Quebec continues to have separate provincial requirements. (Canada)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION A qualifying public-university Canadian master’s currently has a simpler PAL/TAL position than many other study pathways. This is useful when comparing programs, but it should not influence the engineering roadmap.
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION Before the actual study-permit application.
OFFICIAL SOURCE IRCC / Canada.ca. (Canada)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

2. Current PGWP treatment of Master’s degrees
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED Under current rules, a qualifying master’s degree program of at least eight months may support a three-year PGWP even when the master’s itself is shorter than two years, provided all other PGWP eligibility requirements are met. This special master’s treatment has applied since February 15, 2024. (Canada)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION A one-year versus two-year master’s should not be compared on the assumption that a shorter master’s automatically means a shorter PGWP. Career value, cost, research, experience opportunities and exact program eligibility still matter.
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION Before accepting any program offer and again before PGWP application.
OFFICIAL SOURCE IRCC / Canada.ca. (Canada)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

3. PGWP language + field-of-study rules for degree graduates
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED Current PGWP rules require graduates of bachelor’s, master’s and doctoral degrees who are subject to the post-November-1-2024 rules to demonstrate at least CLB 7 / NCLC 7 in all four language abilities. Degree graduates currently have no PGWP field-of-study requirement. (Canada)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION ATLAS must keep university-admission English requirements separate from PGWP language requirements. A Computer Science/AI master’s currently does not depend on the PGWP eligible-field list that applies to certain non-degree programs.
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION Before selecting the eventual language test and again before PGWP application.
OFFICIAL SOURCE IRCC / Canada.ca. (Canada)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

4. Current off-campus student work limit
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED Eligible international students can currently work up to 24 hours per week off campus during regular academic terms and may work unlimited hours during qualifying scheduled academic breaks, subject to the study-permit eligibility conditions. (Canada)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION Part-time work may provide useful experience or income, but ATLAS must not build Canadian affordability around assumed student employment.
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION Before travel, after receiving the study permit, and whenever work conditions materially change.
OFFICIAL SOURCE IRCC / Canada.ca. (Canada)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

5. DLI status alone does not guarantee PGWP eligibility
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED A school being a DLI is not by itself enough. IRCC explicitly states that not every program at a DLI is PGWP-eligible, and students must also satisfy the other PGWP eligibility requirements. (Canada)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION
ATLAS must evaluate:
Exact university + exact program + exact credential + exact route
rather than storing:
“University X = PGWP eligible.”
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION Before paying a substantial deposit or accepting an offer.
OFFICIAL SOURCE IRCC DLI list / Canada.ca. (Canada)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

6. Current study-permit financial-support baseline
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED For a single applicant outside Quebec, IRCC currently lists CAD 22,895 as the first-year living-expense amount for study-permit applications submitted on or after September 1, 2025. This excludes tuition and transportation, and IRCC states these amounts are updated each year. Applicants must show first-year resources and, for longer programs, explain how the full program will be financed. (Canada)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION This is a study-permit evidence threshold, not a prediction of actual Thunder Bay living costs and not the complete cost of education.
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION High priority. Recheck whenever IRCC publishes the next annual amount and definitely before a study-permit application.
OFFICIAL SOURCE IRCC / Canada.ca. (Canada)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

7. Major 2026 Ontario immigration change
CURRENTLY CONFIRMED Ontario's former Master’s Graduate stream closed May 30, 2026. In June 2026 Ontario began a broader OINP redesign: the previous eight streams were removed and the new Ontario Workforce Priority model introduced. The current Workforce Priority route is built around qualifying Ontario employment/job offers for skilled foreign workers, aside from its separate physician provisions. (Ontario)
STRATEGIC IMPLICATION
ATLAS must mark the old assumption:
Ontario master’s → OINP Master’s Graduate pathway
as:
SUPERSEDED
An Ontario master’s may still have career, education and future immigration value, but ATLAS must not claim that completing one currently creates the old graduate-stream pathway.
REVERIFY BEFORE ACTION Ontario is still implementing its redesigned system. Revalidate during university shortlisting and before any immigration planning.
OFFICIAL SOURCE Government of Ontario / OINP. (Ontario)
LAST VERIFIED 12 August 2026.

B. Preliminary alternative programs
1. University of Ottawa — MCS + Concentration in Applied Artificial Intelligence
Why it belongs: Strong direct comparison for an AI-focused Computer Science master's with both research and project routes and current co-op availability.
The current Applied AI concentration includes Machine Learning and AI Ethics plus specialized AI coursework; both thesis and coursework/project routes exist. Co-op is currently available to eligible students in those routes and is competitive. Minimum published admission is a B/70% honours Computer Science degree or equivalent. (uOttawa Catalogue)
ADMISSION COMPETITIVENESS / PROFILE FIT: COMPETITIVE
Reason: the numerical minimum is reachable, but Computer Engineering equivalency, CS-course depth, program competition and—in the thesis route—supervisor fit matter. The university itself states that the minimum does not guarantee admission. (uOttawa Catalogue)
Major advantages: explicit Applied AI curriculum; thesis/project choice; co-op; software/systems coursework remains part of the program.
Major trade-offs: likely stronger admission competition; Ottawa cost and tuition need full normalization later; thesis requires supervisor alignment.
Deeper research later? YES — strong candidate for core/ambitious comparison.

2. University of Guelph — MSc Computer Science + Collaborative Specialization in AI
Why it belongs: Strong research-oriented AI alternative.
The MSc CS is a two-year thesis program. Guelph's AI collaborative specialization adds ML/deep learning/AI research and is Vector-affiliated. Current MSc CS admissions require at least 75% over the previous two years, and applicants from another discipline need substantial equivalent CS preparation; securing a faculty research advisor is mandatory. (University of Guelph)
A notable current funding advantage is that admitted MSc CS students who secure a graduate faculty advisor are listed with a minimum CAD 20,000 annual funding stipend for up to two years. The AI specialization is also currently eligible for Vector Scholarship consideration; the scholarship is not guaranteed. (University of Guelph)
ADMISSION COMPETITIVENESS / PROFILE FIT: COMPETITIVE
Reason: academics may be compatible, but research alignment, supervisor availability and equivalent CS coursework create substantial uncertainty.
Major advantages: deeper AI research environment; thesis experience; guaranteed minimum program funding under the stated conditions; Vector-affiliated AI ecosystem. (University of Guelph)
Major trade-offs: research-heavy; supervisor-dependent; less appropriate if a professional/co-op-oriented master's becomes preferable.
Deeper research later? YES — strong research/AI alternative.

3. University of Manitoba — MSc Computer Science
Why it belongs: Strong value/research comparison outside Ontario.
The current program is a two-year thesis-based MSc, with graduate offerings including Advanced Machine Learning and Networked Systems for Machine Learning. Applicants need a four-year CS degree with suitable coursework, must secure supervisor support before applying, and the university's general graduate minimum is a B/3.0-equivalent in the relevant recent study period. (University of Manitoba)
The department currently states that newly admitted MSc CS students receive minimum funding of CAD 20,000 per year for the first two years, from combinations of grants, scholarships and academic employment. (University of Manitoba)
ADMISSION COMPETITIVENESS / PROFILE FIT: COMPETITIVE
Reason: the major uncertainty is Computer Engineering → required CS-course equivalency plus supervisor support, not merely GPA.
Major advantages: funded research-oriented route; current ML/systems coursework; potentially strong value proposition.
Major trade-offs: thesis/supervisor dependency; Winnipeg rather than Ontario; less suitable if the eventual goal becomes a purely professional/course-based master's.
Deeper research later? YES — useful value/research alternative.

4. University of Windsor — Master of Applied Computing, Artificial Intelligence Stream
Why it belongs: Useful contrast against research-oriented programs because this is a professional applied-computing pathway.
Current admission requires a four-year Computer Science or related degree, with a minimum 70% major average or 77% over the stated recent study period. The AI stream also requires a technology-project statement, interview and two references. It includes an approved work-placement internship, with an on-campus supervised project used in exceptional cases where placement is unavailable. (University of Windsor)
The broader MAC is currently an in-person, four-consecutive-term / 16-month professional program, beginning in September or January. Windsor describes it as self-funded; its current information says GA positions and university scholarships are not available to students in this professional program. (University of Windsor)
ADMISSION COMPETITIVENESS / PROFILE FIT: REALISTIC — PROVISIONAL
Reason: a Computer Engineering background plus substantial software/project evidence is directionally compatible with the “related discipline” requirement, but current transcript/core-CS-course review is still required, and Windsor explicitly notes high application volume. (University of Windsor)
Major advantages: professional orientation; explicit AI stream; internship component; strong alignment if employment transition becomes more important than thesis research.
Major trade-offs: self-funded; less research depth; no normal GA/scholarship support for MAC; internship placement is not absolutely guaranteed.
Deeper research later? YES — useful professional/core alternative.
